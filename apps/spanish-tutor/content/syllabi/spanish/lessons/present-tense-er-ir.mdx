---
title: "Present tense: -er and -ir verbs"
summary: Comer and vivir — the same shape as -ar, with two endings swapped.
readingMinutes: 20
---

The -ar family gave you six endings. The other two families, **-er** and **-ir**, use six endings
of their own — but they are so close to each other, and so close to -ar, that this lesson is mostly
about noticing what *doesn't* change. By the end of it, every regular verb in Spanish is within reach.

## 1. Comer and vivir, next to hablar

<Compare
  columns={['', 'hablar', 'comer — to eat', 'vivir — to live']}
  rows={[
    ['yo', 'hablo', 'como', 'vivo'],
    ['tú', 'hablas', 'comes', 'vives'],
    ['él / ella / usted', 'habla', 'come', 'vive'],
    ['nosotros', 'hablamos', 'comemos', 'vivimos'],
    ['vosotros', 'habláis', 'coméis', 'vivís'],
    ['ellos / ellas / ustedes', 'hablan', 'comen', 'viven'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[2, 3]}
  caption="Read down the yo row: -o, -o, -o. The yo form is the same in all three families. Then read across each row: where -ar has an a, -er and -ir have an e."
/>

<GrammarPoint pattern="como · comes · come · comemos · coméis · comen" title="-er endings" speak={null}>

-o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. Take the -ar endings and swap every a for an e; that is the whole
difference.

</GrammarPoint>

<GrammarPoint pattern="vivo · vives · vive · vivimos · vivís · viven" title="-ir endings" speak={null}>

-o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en. Identical to -er in four rows out of six. Only nosotros and
vosotros differ: -imos and -ís instead of -emos and -éis.

</GrammarPoint>

<Callout kind="note" title="The two swapped endings">
That is the entire -er / -ir distinction: comemos vs. vivimos, coméis vs. vivís. Everywhere else
the two families are the same verb pattern wearing two labels. If you remember that -ir keeps its i
in the we and you-plural rows, you have it.
</Callout>

<Predict
  question="vivir — to live. How do you say 'we live'?"
  options={['vivemos', 'vivimos', 'vivamos', 'viven']}
  answer={1}
>
  vivimos — the -ir family keeps its i in the nosotros row. vivemos would be the -er ending on an
  -ir verb, which is the one mistake this lesson exists to prevent.
</Predict>

/>

<Callout kind="warning" title="comes is not English comes">
The tú form of comer is comes — "you eat". It is pure coincidence that it is also an English word.
Spanish has no verb that looks like "come" and means arrive; that is venir, in the next lesson's
neighbourhood.
</Callout>
