A small number of fixed phrases cover most of what you say at the start and end of any
interaction. None of them need grammar yet — they're said as whole units, the way "thanks" or "see
you" are in English. One of them, though, is the first place the agreement rule from the last
lesson shows up in everyday speech.

## 1. Hello — and the three greetings that depend on the clock

<VocabCard word="hola" meaning="hello / hi" pronunciation="OH-la — the h is silent" note="Works at any time of day, with anyone. The one greeting you can never get wrong." />

Alongside hola, Spanish greets by time of day — and these are used far more than English "good
morning" is:

<Compare
  columns={['Greeting', 'When', 'Literally']}
  rows={[
    ['buenos días', 'morning, until about lunchtime', 'good days'],
    ['buenas tardes', 'afternoon and early evening', 'good afternoons'],
    ['buenas noches', 'evening and night — as a greeting, not only at bedtime', 'good nights'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
  caption="All three are plural in Spanish — 'good days' rather than 'good day' — which is just how the phrases are fixed."
/>

<Callout kind="why" title="buenos días but buenas tardes — agreement, live">
Look at the endings. buen**os** días, but buen**as** tardes and buen**as** noches. día is one of
the masculine -a nouns from the gender lesson, so its adjective is masculine: buenos. tarde and
noche are feminine, so theirs is feminine: buenas. You've been handed the agreement rule inside a
phrase you'll say every day — which is the best possible place to meet it.
</Callout>

<Predict
  question="It's 8 p.m. and you're walking into a restaurant. What do you say?"
  options={['buenos días', 'buenas tardes', 'buenas noches', 'adiós']}
  answer={2}
>
  buenas noches. It is a greeting for the evening, not only a "good night" said on the way to bed
  — English speakers tend to hold it back for too long. (At 8 p.m. some speakers would still say
  buenas tardes; both are heard, and nobody will blink.)
</Predict>

## 4. Please, sorry, and excuse me

<Compare
  columns={['Phrase', 'Means', 'Use it for']}
  rows={[
    ['por favor', 'please', 'any request'],
    ['perdón', 'sorry / excuse me', 'small things — bumping into someone, getting past, getting attention'],
    ['disculpe', 'excuse me (formal)', 'getting a stranger\'s attention politely — a waiter, a passer-by'],
    ['lo siento', "I'm sorry", 'genuine regret or sympathy — bad news, a real apology'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
  caption="perdón and lo siento are not interchangeable. perdón is the light one; lo siento literally means 'I feel it' and carries weight."
/>

<Predict
  question="You bump into someone on a bus. Which fits?"
  options={['lo siento', 'perdón', 'de nada', 'hasta luego']}
  answer={1}
>
  perdón — a small, immediate apology. lo siento would sound as if you'd done them real harm; it
  belongs with bad news, not a jostle.
</Predict>

<Callout kind="note" title="Every one of these is written with its marks">
¡Hola! ¡Buenos días! ¡Hasta luego! — the opening ¡ is not optional in writing, as the
pronunciation lesson said. Greetings are mostly exclamations, so this is where you'll write it
most.
</Callout>
