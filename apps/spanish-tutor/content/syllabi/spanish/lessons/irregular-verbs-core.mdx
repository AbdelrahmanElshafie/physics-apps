---
title: The irregulars you cannot avoid
summary: Tener, ir, querer, hacer — the handful of irregular verbs that appear in almost every conversation.
readingMinutes: 25
---

You now conjugate every regular verb in the language. The bad news is that the most common verbs
are not regular — Spanish, like English, has worn its everyday verbs smooth in odd places. The
good news is that four of them cover an enormous share of real conversation, and that they are
irregular in only three different ways. Learn the four tables and the three ways, and the rest of
the irregulars will look familiar when they come.

## 1. Tener — to have

<Compare
  columns={['', 'tener', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['yo', 'tengo', 'I have'],
    ['tú', 'tienes', 'you have'],
    ['él / ella / usted', 'tiene', 'he / she has · you (formal) have'],
    ['nosotros', 'tenemos', 'we have'],
    ['vosotros', 'tenéis', 'you (plural, Spain) have'],
    ['ellos / ellas / ustedes', 'tienen', 'they have · you (plural) have'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[1]}
  caption="Two things going on: the yo form grows a g (tengo), and e becomes ie in tienes, tiene, tienen — but not in tenemos or tenéis."
/>

Tener does a job English gives to "to be". You *have* hunger, thirst, cold, and years:

<Compare
  columns={['Spanish', 'Means', 'Literally']}
  rows={[
    ['Tengo hambre.', "I'm hungry.", 'I have hunger'],
    ['Tengo sed.', "I'm thirsty.", 'I have thirst'],
    ['Tengo frío.', "I'm cold.", 'I have cold'],
    ['Tengo veinte años.', "I'm twenty (years old).", 'I have twenty years'],
    ['Tengo que trabajar.', 'I have to work.', 'I have that to-work'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
  caption="Age is the one that trips everyone: ¿Cuántos años tienes? — how many years do you have? Never soy veinte."
/>

<VocabCard word="hambre" meaning="hunger — tengo hambre, I'm hungry" />
<VocabCard word="años" meaning="years — tengo veinte años, I'm twenty" note="The ñ matters: anos without it is a different, anatomical word. Spanish speakers enjoy this mistake very much." />
<VocabCard word="tener que + infinitive" meaning="to have to — tengo que trabajar, I have to work" />

## 2. Ir — to go

<Compare
  columns={['', 'ir', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['yo', 'voy', 'I go'],
    ['tú', 'vas', 'you go'],
    ['él / ella / usted', 'va', 'he / she goes · you (formal) go'],
    ['nosotros', 'vamos', 'we go'],
    ['vosotros', 'vais', 'you (plural, Spain) go'],
    ['ellos / ellas / ustedes', 'van', 'they go · you (plural) go'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[1]}
  caption="Nothing of ir survives in its own forms — they all start with v. This is the fully irregular kind: learn it as a list, the way you learned ser."
/>

<GrammarPoint pattern="Voy al mercado." title="ir a + place, and a + el = al">

ir takes a: voy a Madrid, voy a casa. When the place has el in front, a and el fuse into al — never
a el. Voy al mercado, "I'm going to the market". (a la stays two words: voy a la escuela.)

</GrammarPoint>

<Callout kind="note" title="ir a + infinitive: the future you already have">
Voy a comer — "I'm going to eat". Vamos a estudiar — "we're going to study". ir a plus an
infinitive is how everyday Spanish talks about the near future, and it needs no new tense. Phase 4
gives you a past tense; this gives you the future for free.
</Callout>

<VocabCard word="mercado" meaning="market (masculine) — el mercado" />
<VocabCard word="también" meaning="also, too" />

## 3. Querer — to want

<Compare
  columns={['', 'querer', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['yo', 'quiero', 'I want'],
    ['tú', 'quieres', 'you want'],
    ['él / ella / usted', 'quiere', 'he / she wants · you (formal) want'],
    ['nosotros', 'queremos', 'we want'],
    ['vosotros', 'queréis', 'you (plural, Spain) want'],
    ['ellos / ellas / ustedes', 'quieren', 'they want · you (plural) want'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[1]}
  caption="The endings are the ordinary -er endings. Only the stem changes: e → ie, in every row except nosotros and vosotros."
/>

<Callout kind="note" title="The boot">
Write the six forms in two columns — singular on the left, plural on the right — and shade the ones
with ie: all three on the left, only the bottom one on the right. The shaded shape looks like a
boot, and generations of learners have called this the boot pattern. tener follows it too (tienes,
tiene, tienen), and so will dozens of verbs later: poder (o → ue), pedir (e → i).
</Callout>

Querer takes a noun or an infinitive, and it is also the plain way to say "I love you":

<Compare
  columns={['Spanish', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['Quiero un café.', 'I want a coffee.'],
    ['Quiero comer.', 'I want to eat.'],
    ['¿Quieres ir al mercado?', 'Do you want to go to the market?'],
    ['Te quiero.', 'I love you.'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
/>

## 4. Hacer — to do, to make

<Compare
  columns={['', 'hacer', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['yo', 'hago', 'I do / make'],
    ['tú', 'haces', 'you do'],
    ['él / ella / usted', 'hace', 'he / she does · you (formal) do'],
    ['nosotros', 'hacemos', 'we do'],
    ['vosotros', 'hacéis', 'you (plural, Spain) do'],
    ['ellos / ellas / ustedes', 'hacen', 'they do · you (plural) do'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[1]}
  caption="The gentlest kind of irregular: only the yo form misbehaves (hago, not haco). Every other row is a regular -er verb."
/>

<Compare
  columns={['Spanish', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['¿Qué haces?', 'What are you doing?'],
    ['Hace frío.', "It's cold. (weather)"],
    ['Hace calor.', "It's hot. (weather)"],
    ['Hace sol.', "It's sunny."],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
  caption="Weather is hace — literally 'it makes cold'. So Spanish has tener for how a person feels (tengo frío) and hacer for the weather (hace frío), and never estar for either."
/>

## 5. Three kinds of irregular

<Compare
  columns={['Kind', 'Verb', 'What changes']}
  rows={[
    ['yo-form only', 'hacer', 'hago — then regular'],
    ['stem change (the boot)', 'querer', 'e → ie except nosotros / vosotros'],
    ['both at once', 'tener', 'tengo, then tienes / tiene / tienen'],
    ['fully irregular', 'ir (and ser, estar)', 'a list — learn it whole'],
  ]}
  caption="Almost every irregular verb you meet later will be one of these kinds. The question to ask of a new one is not 'is it irregular?' but 'which kind?'."
/>

<Predict
  question="How do you say 'I'm hungry'?"
  options={['Soy hambre', 'Estoy hambre', 'Tengo hambre', 'Hago hambre']}
  answer={2}
>
  Tengo hambre — hunger is something you have. Neither "to be" is used, and hacer is for the weather.
</Predict>
