---
title: Word stress and written accents
summary: Two rules predict the stress of almost every word; the written accent marks every exception — and sometimes the only difference between two words.
readingMinutes: 20
---

Every Spanish word of more than one syllable has one stressed syllable, and — unlike in English or
Russian — you never have to guess which. Two rules cover nearly every word, and the written accent
mark (the tilde: á é í ó ú) exists to flag the ones they don't. That makes the accent a piece of
information, not decoration: leave it off and you have written a different word, or no word.

## 1. Rule one: ends in a vowel, n, or s → second-to-last syllable

## 2. Rule two: ends in any other consonant → last syllable

<Predict
  question="Where does the stress fall in the word animal (ends in l)?"
  options={['A-ni-mal', 'a-NI-mal', 'a-ni-MAL', 'Impossible to tell without hearing it']}
  answer={2}
>
  a-ni-MAL. It ends in a consonant other than n or s, so rule two applies and the stress goes on
  the last syllable. No accent mark is written because the rule already gives the right answer.
</Predict>

## 3. The written accent marks every exception

When a word's stress isn't where the rules would put it, an accent is written over the vowel that
*does* carry it. That's the entire function of the mark:

<Compare
  columns={['Word', 'The rule would say', 'The accent says', 'So it is stressed']}
  rows={[
    ['árbol', 'ar-BOL (ends in l → last)', 'no — first syllable', 'ÁR-bol'],
    ['café', 'CA-fe (ends in vowel → second-to-last)', 'no — last syllable', 'ca-FÉ'],
    ['teléfono', 'te-le-FO-no', 'no — second syllable', 'te-LÉ-fo-no'],
    ['música', 'mu-SI-ca', 'no — first syllable', 'MÚ-si-ca'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0]}
  caption="An accent mark always means: ignore the rule, stress this syllable. No accent always means: the rule is right."
/>

<Callout kind="why" title="This is why stress is never a guess">
Put the two rules and the accent together and stress becomes fully predictable from spelling. A
word with an accent is stressed on the accented vowel; a word without one is stressed by rule one
or rule two. There is no third case. This is also why, when you *hear* a word stressed somewhere
the rules wouldn't put it, you already know it must be spelled with an accent.
</Callout>

## 4. When the accent is the whole difference

Because the accent moves the stress, it can turn one word into another. These pairs are spelled
with the same letters:

<AccentDemo
  items={[
    { word: 'hablo', syllables: ['ha', 'blo'], stress: 0, meaning: 'I speak — present tense, stress by rule one' },
    { word: 'habló', syllables: ['ha', 'bló'], stress: 1, meaning: 'he / she spoke — past tense, the accent moves the stress' },
    { word: 'papa', syllables: ['pa', 'pa'], stress: 0, meaning: 'potato' },
    { word: 'papá', syllables: ['pa', 'pá'], stress: 1, meaning: 'dad' },
    { word: 'esta', syllables: ['es', 'ta'], stress: 0, meaning: 'this (feminine)' },
    { word: 'está', syllables: ['es', 'tá'], stress: 1, meaning: 'is — he, she or it is' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout kind="warning" title="hablo / habló is a whole tense">
That first pair is not a curiosity. The present tense "I speak" and the past tense "he spoke"
differ *only* by the accent, and the preterite lesson in Phase 4 depends on your being able to
hear and write that difference. If the stress rules feel abstract now, that pair is why they
matter.
</Callout>

## 5. Accents on one-syllable words

A one-syllable word has nowhere else to put its stress, so an accent on it isn't about stress at
all — it distinguishes two words that would otherwise be spelled identically:

<Compare
  columns={['Without accent', 'Means', 'With accent', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['el', 'the', 'él', 'he'],
    ['si', 'if', 'sí', 'yes'],
    ['tu', 'your', 'tú', 'you'],
    ['que', 'that', 'qué', 'what?'],
  ]}
  speakColumns={[0, 2]}
  caption="Same sound, same stress, different word. The accent is doing exactly one job: telling the two apart on paper."
/>

<Predict
  question="You want to write 'yes'. Which is it?"
  options={['si', 'sí', 'Either — the accent is optional on short words', 'sì']}
  answer={1}
>
  sí. Without the accent, si means "if" — a different word. The accent on one-syllable words is
  never optional; it is the only thing distinguishing them in writing.
</Predict>
