---
title: "Yes/no questions with 吗, and 呢"
summary: Turning any statement into a question by adding one syllable at the end.
readingMinutes: 15
---

Both of these particles have appeared already — 吗 in 你好吗 and 你是中国人吗, 呢 in 你呢. This lesson
makes them official, and adds the thing that makes Mandarin questions genuinely easier than
English ones: nothing in the sentence ever has to move.

## 1. 吗 — statement + 吗 = yes/no question

<GrammarPoint pattern="你是学生。 → 你是学生吗?" title="Add 吗, change nothing else">

你是学生 ("you are a student") becomes 你是学生吗 ("are you a student?") by putting 吗 at the end.
No inversion, no "do", no auxiliary — the word order is identical to the statement. This is what
the word-order lesson meant by "questions don't change anything."

</GrammarPoint>

<Callout kind="note" title="One small adjustment: 很 drops out">
他很忙 ("he is busy") doesn't become 他很忙吗 but **他忙吗** ("is he busy?"). The 很 in the statement
was only ever a placeholder to stop the sentence sounding unfinished; a question doesn't need
the help, so it goes. Keep it only if you genuinely mean "is he *very* busy?"
</Callout>

## 2. Answering: repeat the verb

Mandarin has no all-purpose "yes" and "no" to drop into an answer. You answer by repeating the
key word — the verb or the adjective — either as-is or negated:

<Compare
  columns={['Question', 'Yes', 'No']}
  rows={[
    ['你是学生吗?', '是。', '不是。'],
    ['他忙吗?', '忙。', '不忙。'],
    ['你有书吗?', '有。', '没有。'],
  ]}
  caption="The echo of the verb is the answer. (对 — 'correct' — exists for agreeing with a statement, but it isn't the default way to answer a 吗 question.)"
/>

<Predict
  question="Someone asks 你要茶吗 ('do you want tea?'). What is the natural one-word 'no'?"
  options={['不', '没要', '不要', '没']}
  answer={2}
>
  不要 — the verb 要, negated. A bare 不 on its own isn't an answer; 没 is only for 有 and for
  things that didn't happen, which is the next lesson.
</Predict>

## 3. Question words stay where the answer goes

English drags its question word to the front: "*What* is your name?" Mandarin leaves it in the
slot the answer will occupy:

<Compare
  columns={['Question', 'Answer', 'Notice']}
  rows={[
    ['你叫什么名字?', '我叫王明。', '什么 sits where 王明 will go'],
    ['他是谁?', '他是老师。', '谁 sits where 老师 will go'],
    ['你在哪里?', '我在家。', '哪里 sits where 家 will go'],
  ]}
  caption="Same order as the answer, every time — which is why Mandarin question words are easy to place once you can say the answer."
/>

<VocabCard hanzi="谁" pinyin="shéi" meaning="who" />
<VocabCard hanzi="哪里" pinyin="nǎlǐ" meaning="where" note="哪 (which) + 里 (inside) — 'which place'." />

<Callout kind="warning" title="吗 and a question word don't combine">
你叫什么名字吗 is wrong. 吗 asks for yes or no; 什么 asks for a name. A sentence can't want both at
once, so a question with a question word in it never takes 吗.
</Callout>

## 4. 呢 — bouncing a question back

<VocabCard hanzi="呢" pinyin="ne" meaning="…and what about…?" />

<Compare
  columns={['Mandarin', 'Means', 'What 呢 is doing']}
  rows={[
    ['我很好，你呢?', "I'm fine — and you?", 'repeats the previous question without restating it'],
    ['我的书呢?', "Where's my book? / What about my book?", 'asks after something that should be here and isn\'t'],
  ]}
/>

<Predict
  question="A friend says 我是美国人 and wants to ask your nationality in return. Which is the natural follow-up?"
  options={['你是美国人吗?', '你呢?', '你是什么?', '你吗?']}
  answer={1}
>
  你呢? — "and you?" It reuses the question just asked without repeating it. 你是美国人吗 would be
  grammatical but asks a narrower question ("are *you* American?"), which isn't quite what's meant.
</Predict>
