---
title: Hello, goodbye, and thank you
summary: The handful of phrases that open and close almost every conversation in Mandarin.
readingMinutes: 10
---

A small number of fixed phrases cover most of what you say at the start and end of any
interaction. None of them need grammar yet — they're said as whole units, the way "thanks" or
"see you" are in English.

## 1. Hello

<VocabCard hanzi="你好" pinyin="nǐ hǎo" meaning="hello" note="Literally 'you good' — a fixed greeting, not a question about your wellbeing." />

<VocabCard hanzi="您好" pinyin="nín hǎo" meaning="hello (formal / respectful)" note="您 (nín) is the polite form of 你 (nǐ) — use it for elders, teachers, or anyone you'd address formally." />

<Callout kind="note" title="你好 is an opener, not small talk">
Unlike English "hi, how are you?", 你好 doesn't expect an answer about how you actually are — it's
purely a greeting. The actual "how are you" question (你好吗?) is its own, separate phrase, covered
in a later lesson.
</Callout>

## 2. Thank you, and you're welcome

<VocabCard hanzi="谢谢" pinyin="xièxie" meaning="thank you" note="The second syllable is often said lightly, almost like a neutral tone, even though it's written xiè again." />

<VocabCard hanzi="不客气" pinyin="bú kèqi" meaning="you're welcome" note="Literally 'don't be polite/distant' — the standard reply to 谢谢." />

<Predict
  question="Someone thanks you (谢谢) for holding a door. What's the standard reply?"
  options={['你好', '不客气', '再见', '对不起']}
  answer={1}
>
  不客气 (bú kèqi) is the standard "you're welcome." 你好 is a greeting, 再见 is goodbye, and 对不起 is
  an apology — each is a different fixed phrase for a different social moment.
</Predict>

## 3. Sorry, and excuse me

<VocabCard hanzi="对不起" pinyin="duìbuqǐ" meaning="sorry / I apologize" />

<VocabCard hanzi="不好意思" pinyin="bù hǎoyìsi" meaning="excuse me / sorry (lighter, more casual)" note="Used for small things — bumping into someone, asking a stranger a question — where 对不起 would feel too heavy." />

## 4. Goodbye
