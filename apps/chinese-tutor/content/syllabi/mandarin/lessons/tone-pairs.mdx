The last lesson taught tones as if each syllable stood alone. Real sentences don't work that way —
syllables run together, and a few very common tone combinations shift in completely predictable
ways. This shifting is called **tone sandhi**, and two patterns cover almost everything you'll hit
early on.

<Callout kind="note" title="The spelling doesn't change — only the sound does">
Every sandhi rule in this lesson changes how a syllable is *said*, never how it's *written*. 你好 is
always written nǐ hǎo. What changes is the pitch that actually comes out of your mouth — this
lesson is about pronunciation, not a new spelling to memorize.
</Callout>

## 1. Third tone + third tone

Say two third tones back to back and the dip-then-rise shape of the first one gets flattened out —
it's simply too slow to do twice in a row at conversational speed. The fix: **the first third tone
is said as a second tone instead.**

<GrammarPoint pattern="3rd + 3rd  →  2nd + 3rd" title="Third-tone sandhi">

你好 is written nǐ hǎo (3rd + 3rd), but said as **ní hǎo** — the first syllable rises instead of
dipping, exactly like a second tone. This isn't an exception you memorize word by word; it's a
blanket rule that applies to *any* two third tones in a row, in any word or phrase.

</GrammarPoint>

### The "half third" tone

A third tone *isn't* followed by another third tone far more often than it is — and in that more
common case, it's usually shortened to just the low dip, without the rising tail back up. This
"half third" is what a third tone actually sounds like most of the time in fluent speech; the full
dip-and-rise shape from the first lesson is really the careful, isolated-syllable version.

## 2. The sandhi of 不 (bù), "not"

不 is written with a fourth tone — bù — but changes to **bú** (second tone) whenever the syllable
right after it is *also* fourth tone. You already met this in the greetings lesson without the rule
being named yet:

<Callout kind="forward">
The character `一` (yī, "one") has a third sandhi pattern of its own, shifting tone depending on
