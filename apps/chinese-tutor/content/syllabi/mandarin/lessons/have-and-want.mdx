---
title: "有 (to have) and 要 (to want)"
summary: Two of the most-used verbs in the language, and how they build simple sentences.
readingMinutes: 15
---

Everything so far has been 是 — linking one noun to another. These two verbs are the first that
actually *do* something to their object, and between them they cover a remarkable share of
everyday speech: having things, wanting things, and wanting to do things.

## 1. 有 — to have

<VocabCard hanzi="有" pinyin="yǒu" meaning="to have" />

<GrammarPoint pattern="我有两本书。" title="Subject + 有 + (number + measure word +) noun">

我有两本书 — "I have two books." The object follows the usual counting pattern from the
measure-words lesson: 两 (the counting-things two), 本 (the measure word for books), 书.

</GrammarPoint>

<Callout kind="warning" title="有 is negated with 没, never 不">
Every other verb you'll meet is negated by putting 不 in front of it. 有 is the one exception: "I
don't have" is **没有** (méiyǒu), and 不有 simply does not exist. This is worth fixing now, before
the negation lesson, because 没有 comes up constantly — it's also the standard answer to "do you
have…?"
</Callout>

<VocabCard hanzi="没有" pinyin="méiyǒu" meaning="to not have — the only negation 有 takes" />
<VocabCard hanzi="钱" pinyin="qián" meaning="money" />

<Dialogue
  caption="Having and not having"
  lines={[
    { speaker: 'A', hanzi: '你有书吗?', pinyin: 'Nǐ yǒu shū ma?', meaning: 'Do you have a book?' },
    { speaker: 'B', hanzi: '我没有书，我有钱。', pinyin: 'Wǒ méiyǒu shū, wǒ yǒu qián.', meaning: "I don't have a book — I have money." },
  ]}
/>

## 2. 要 — to want

<VocabCard hanzi="要" pinyin="yào" meaning="to want; (before a verb) want to / going to" />
<VocabCard hanzi="茶" pinyin="chá" meaning="tea" />
<VocabCard hanzi="吃" pinyin="chī" meaning="to eat" />

<Compare
  columns={['Pattern', 'Example', 'Means']}
  rows={[
    ['要 + noun', '我要一杯茶。', 'I want a cup of tea.'],
    ['要 + verb', '我要吃。', "I want to eat / I'm going to eat."],
  ]}
  caption="Followed by a noun, 要 is 'want'. Followed by a verb, it shades between 'want to' and 'am going to' — context decides, and the two often amount to the same thing."
/>

<Predict
  question="How do you say 'I don't want it' — 不要 or 没要?"
  options={['没要 — like 没有', '不要 — 要 is a normal verb, only 有 takes 没', 'Either works', 'Neither — 要 cannot be negated']}
  answer={1}
>
  不要. The 没 rule is specific to 有; 要 negates with 不 like every other verb. And since 要 is
  fourth tone, that 不 is pronounced bú — bú yào — by the sandhi rule from the tone-pairs lesson.
</Predict>

<VocabCard hanzi="不要" pinyin="bú yào" meaning="to not want; don't (want to)" note="bú, not bù — 要 is fourth tone." />

## 3. Finally: what 一 does in front of a measure word

The tone-pairs lesson promised that 一 (yī, "one") has a sandhi pattern of its own, waiting for a
lesson with measure words in it. This is that lesson.

<Compare
  columns={['Next syllable', '一 becomes', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['fourth tone', 'yí (second tone)', '一个人 yí gè rén — one person'],
    ['first, second or third tone', 'yì (fourth tone)', '一本书 yì běn shū — one book · 一杯茶 yì bēi chá — one cup of tea'],
    ['(standing alone, or when counting)', 'yī (first tone)', '一, 二, 三…'],
  ]}
  caption="Same shape as the 不 rule: the written character never changes, only the tone that comes out."
/>

<Predict
  question="一 in front of 杯 (bēi, first tone) is pronounced..."
  options={['yī', 'yí', 'yì', 'yi (neutral)']}
  answer={2}
>
  yì — before a first, second or third tone, 一 takes the fourth tone. Only before another fourth
  tone (like 个) does it become yí.
</Predict>
