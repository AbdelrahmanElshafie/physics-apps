title: Asking how someone is
summary: 你好吗? and the adjective-as-predicate pattern that trips up English speakers.
readingMinutes: 15
---

The introducing-yourself lesson ended on a warning: 是 is for nouns, never adjectives, and "I am
tired" would have to work some other way. This is the other way — and it's simpler than the
warning made it sound, because the adjective just does the job by itself.

## 1. The question

<VocabCard hanzi="你好吗?" pinyin="Nǐ hǎo ma?" meaning="How are you? (lit. 'you good [question]?')" note="吗 (ma) turns the statement 你好 into a yes/no question — it gets its own lesson in Phase 5. Note it's the neutral-tone 吗 from the tone-comparison table, not 妈 or 马." />

<Callout kind="note" title="Textbook-correct, slightly textbook-sounding">
你好吗 is perfectly understood and the right place to start. Native speakers between themselves
more often ask things like 最近怎么样 ("how have things been lately?") — but that needs vocabulary
you don't have yet. Learn this one; you'll outgrow it naturally.
</Callout>

## 2. The answer — with no verb in it

<GrammarPoint pattern="我很好。" title="Subject + 很 + adjective">

我很好 — "I'm fine." There is no 是 here, and there can't be: 好 is an adjective, and in Mandarin an
adjective **is** the predicate. It doesn't need a linking verb the way English "am fine" does.

</GrammarPoint>

<Compare
  columns={['What follows the subject', 'Pattern', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['a noun', 'Subject + 是 + noun', '我是学生 — I am a student'],
    ['an adjective', 'Subject + 很 + adjective', '我很累 — I am tired'],
  ]}
  caption="Two different sentence shapes for what English treats as one verb. Which one you need is decided entirely by whether the second word is a noun or an adjective."
/>

<VocabCard hanzi="很" pinyin="hěn" meaning="very — but see below" />
<VocabCard hanzi="累" pinyin="lèi" meaning="tired" />
<VocabCard hanzi="忙" pinyin="máng" meaning="busy" />

<Callout kind="warning" title="很 usually doesn't mean 'very' here">
Dictionaries say 很 means "very," and on its own it does. But in the Subject + 很 + adjective
pattern it's mostly a grammatical placeholder: 我很好 means "I'm fine," not "I'm *very* fine." The
reason it's there at all is that a bare adjective after a subject (我好) sounds unfinished — as
if you're about to contrast it with something ("I'm fine, *but*…"). 很 fills the slot and makes
the sentence feel complete. If you genuinely mean "very," you say it with extra emphasis, or use
a stronger word.
</Callout>

<Predict
  question="Which is correct for 'He is busy'?"
  options={['他是忙', '他很忙', '他忙是', '他是很忙']}
  answer={1}
>
  他很忙. 忙 is an adjective, so 是 is wrong — and the 很 is what keeps the sentence from sounding
  like it's trailing off into a "but".
</Predict>

## 4. Returning the question: 呢

<VocabCard hanzi="你呢?" pinyin="Nǐ ne?" meaning="And you? / What about you?" note="呢 bounces the previous question back without repeating it — also covered properly in Phase 5, alongside 吗." />
