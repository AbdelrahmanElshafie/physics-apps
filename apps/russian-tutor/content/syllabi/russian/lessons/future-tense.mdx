Aspect doesn't just affect meaning in the abstract — it decides which of **two completely
different future tenses** you build. There's no single "future tense" in Russian the way there's
a single past tense; which one you use depends entirely on whether the verb is imperfective or
perfective.

## 1. Imperfective future: буду + infinitive

For imperfective verbs (ongoing, habitual, repeated actions), the future is built from a
conjugated form of быть ("to be") plus the imperfective infinitive — two separate words:

<Compare
  columns={['Pronoun', 'буду form']}
  rows={[
    ['я', 'бу́ду'],
    ['ты', 'бу́дешь'],
    ['он/она', 'бу́дет'],
    ['мы', 'бу́дем'],
    ['вы', 'бу́дете'],
    ['они', 'бу́дут'],
  ]}
  caption="буду conjugates like a normal first-conjugation verb — only the stem (irregular буд-) is unusual."
/>

<GrammarPoint pattern="Я бу́ду чита́ть." title="буду + infinitive = imperfective future">

Я буду читать — "I will read / I will be reading." читать stays in its plain infinitive form; all
the conjugation work happens on буду, which changes with the subject the infinitive itself never
does.

</GrammarPoint>

<Predict
  question="Они будут говорить. What is being conjugated for они — говорить or будут?"
  options={['говорить', 'будут', 'Both equally', 'Neither — this is a fixed phrase']}
  answer={1}
>
  будут. The infinitive говорить never changes form here; the conjugation for each pronoun happens
  entirely on буду/будешь/будет/будем/будете/будут.
</Predict>

## 2. Perfective future: conjugate the perfective verb directly

For perfective verbs (single, completed actions), there's no auxiliary at all — you conjugate the
perfective verb exactly the way you conjugated an imperfective *present*-tense verb:

<GrammarPoint pattern="я прочита́ю  ·  ты прочита́ешь  ·  они́ прочита́ют" title="Perfective future: same endings, no буду">

прочитать conjugates я прочитаю, ты прочитаешь, он прочитает, мы прочитаем, вы прочитаете, они
прочитают — identical endings to читать's present tense, just on the perfective stem прочита-, and
meaning something completely different: one future action that *will be finished*, not an ongoing
present one.

</GrammarPoint>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Same-looking conjugation, two different meanings depending on the verb">
This is worth sitting with: читаю (imperfective present: "I read/am reading") and прочитаю
(perfective future: "I will [completely] read") are conjugated identically — same six endings,
same pattern. The *verb itself* (imperfective vs. perfective) is what tells you which meaning
you're looking at, not anything in the ending.
</Callout>

## 3. Choosing the right future

<Compare
  columns={['You mean...', 'Use', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['An ongoing or repeated future action', 'буду + imperfective infinitive', 'Я буду читать книгу. (I will be reading / I will read, as an activity)'],
    ['One specific action, completed', 'perfective verb, conjugated directly', 'Я прочитаю книгу. (I will finish reading the book)'],
  ]}
  caption="The same choice you learned for aspect in general — process vs. completed result — just projected into the future instead of the present or past."
/>

<Predict
  question="You want to say 'I will read this book tonight' meaning you'll finish it. Which should you use?"
  options={['Я буду читать', 'Я прочитаю', 'Either works identically', 'Neither — use the past tense']}
  answer={1}
>
  Я прочитаю — finishing the book is a single completed result, exactly what the perfective future
  is for. Я буду читать would suggest ongoing/repeated reading without committing to finishing it.
</Predict>
