---
title: The past tense
summary: Past tense agrees with gender, not person — a different agreement rule than the present tense uses.
readingMinutes: 15
---

The present tense agreed with *person* — я, ты, он each got a different ending. The past tense
throws that rule out entirely and agrees with **gender** instead. This is a genuinely different
agreement system, not a variation on the one you already know.

## 1. Forming the past tense

Drop -ть from the infinitive and add one of four endings, depending on the subject:

<Compare
  columns={['Subject', 'Ending', 'читать → ']}
  rows={[
    ['masculine singular', '-л', 'читал'],
    ['feminine singular', '-ла', 'читала'],
    ['neuter singular', '-ло', 'читало'],
    ['any plural', '-ли', 'читали'],
  ]}
  caption="Notice there is no я/ты/он column at all — every singular masculine subject uses читал, whether it's я, ты, or он."
/>

<VocabCard word="читала" stressed="чита́ла" meaning="read (feminine singular past)" />

<GrammarPoint pattern="Он чита́л. / Она́ чита́ла." title="Same verb, same person, different ending">

Он читал / Она читала — both mean "he/she read," and both are third person singular, the
category that got a single shared ending in the present tense. In the past tense they're
different words, because он is masculine and она is feminine.

</GrammarPoint>

<Predict
  question="Я читал vs. Я читала — both are first person (я). What actually decides which one is correct?"
  options={['Random choice — either works for anyone', "The speaker's own gender", 'Whether the book was finished', 'The time of day']}
  answer={1}
>
  The speaker's own gender. я itself has no built-in gender — a man says Я читал, a woman says Я
  читала. The past tense is agreeing with whoever the subject actually is, not with the pronoun.
</Predict>

## 2. Plural: one form for everyone

Unlike the singular (three different endings for three genders), the plural collapses to a single
ending regardless of gender:

<GrammarPoint pattern="Мы чита́ли. Они́ говори́ли." title="-ли for every plural subject">

Мы читали ("we read") and Они говорили ("they spoke") both use the plural ending no matter what
mix of genders is in the group — unlike the singular, plural subjects never need to specify gender
here.

</GrammarPoint>

## 3. быть ("to be") in the past — finally explained

Back in the introducing-yourself lesson, была appeared as "a past-tense form of быть" without the
full pattern being taught yet. Here it is:

<Compare
  columns={['Subject', 'быть (past)']}
  rows={[
    ['masculine singular', 'был'],
    ['feminine singular', 'была'],
    ['neuter singular', 'было'],
    ['any plural', 'были'],
  ]}
  caption="быть follows the exact same four-way gender/plural pattern as any other verb in the past tense — it isn't a special exception here, only in the present tense (where it has no form at all)."
/>

<GrammarPoint pattern="Он был врач. Она была́ врач." title="Finally: 'to be' with a real verb">

Он был врач / Она была врач — "He was a doctor" / "She was a doctor." Compare this to Он врач / Она
врач in the present tense, with no verb at all — был/была is exactly the word the present tense
was missing.

</GrammarPoint>

<Predict
  question={'Это было хорошо ("That was good") — это is grammatically neuter. Why было, not был or была?'}
  options={['It\'s a random exception', "это counts as neuter, so the neuter past-tense ending applies", 'было is used for all abstract statements', "It's a typo for было"]}
  answer={1}
>
  это is treated as grammatically neuter, so it takes the neuter past ending -о (было), the same
  pattern any neuter subject (like окно) would trigger.
</Predict>
