---
title: Present tense conjugation
summary: Two conjugation patterns cover almost every verb you'll meet early on.
readingMinutes: 25
---

Unlike "to be," action verbs like "read" or "speak" are very much present in the present
tense — and they change their ending for every person, the way Russian nouns change theirs for
every case. This lesson introduces the subject pronouns properly, then both conjugation patterns.

## 1. Subject pronouns

<Compare
  columns={['Pronoun', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['я', 'I'],
    ['ты', 'you (singular, informal)'],
    ['он / она / оно', 'he / she / it'],
    ['мы', 'we'],
    ['вы', 'you (plural, or singular formal)'],
    ['они', 'they'],
  ]}
/>

<Callout kind="note" title="вы does two jobs, same split as привет/здравствуйте">
вы is both "you-plural" and the formal "you" for a single person you'd address respectfully —
exactly the same formality split Phase 2 taught for привет (casual) vs. здравствуйте (formal).
Addressing one stranger or an elder as "ты" would be the grammatical equivalent of calling your
boss by a childhood nickname.
</Callout>

## 2. First conjugation: verbs like читать (to read)

Drop the -ть from the infinitive, and add these endings:

<Compare
  columns={['Pronoun', 'Ending', 'читать → ']}
  rows={[
    ['я', '-ю', 'читаю'],
    ['ты', '-ешь', 'читаешь'],
    ['он/она', '-ет', 'читает'],
    ['мы', '-ем', 'читаем'],
    ['вы', '-ете', 'читаете'],
    ['они', '-ют', 'читают'],
  ]}
  caption="Most verbs ending in -ать or -ять in the infinitive follow this pattern."
/>

<VocabCard word="читаю" stressed="чита́ю" meaning="I read / I am reading" />

<GrammarPoint pattern="Я чита́ю кни́гу." title="First conjugation in action">

Я читаю книгу — "I read/am reading a book." Russian doesn't distinguish "I read" (habitual) from
"I am reading" (right now) with a different verb form the way English does — one present-tense
form covers both; context tells you which is meant.

</GrammarPoint>

## 3. Second conjugation: verbs like говорить (to speak)

Drop the -ить, and add these endings instead:

<Compare
  columns={['Pronoun', 'Ending', 'говорить → ']}
  rows={[
    ['я', '-ю', 'говорю'],
    ['ты', '-ишь', 'говоришь'],
    ['он/она', '-ит', 'говорит'],
    ['мы', '-им', 'говорим'],
    ['вы', '-ите', 'говорите'],
    ['они', '-ят', 'говорят'],
  ]}
  caption="Most verbs ending in -ить in the infinitive follow this pattern."
/>

<VocabCard word="говоришь" stressed="говори́шь" meaning="you speak (informal singular)" />

<GrammarPoint pattern="Ты говори́шь по-ру́сски?" title="Second conjugation in action">

Ты говоришь по-русски? — "Do you speak Russian?" Same idea as first conjugation, different vowel
in every ending: и instead of е, я/а instead of ю/у in the я- and они-forms.

</GrammarPoint>

<Predict
  question="говорить is a second-conjugation verb. What ending would you expect for мы (we)?"
  options={['-ем, like читать', '-им', '-ете', '-ите']}
  answer={1}
>
  -им — говорим. Second conjugation uses и throughout (-ишь, -ит, -им, -ите), where first
  conjugation uses е (-ешь, -ет, -ем, -ете). Mixing the two vowel sets is the most common beginner
  slip.
</Predict>

## 4. Telling the two patterns apart

The infinitive ending is your first clue, though it isn't airtight:
