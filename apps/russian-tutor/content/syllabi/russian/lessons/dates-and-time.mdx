This is the last topic in the course, and most of it is vocabulary. The one piece of grammar in
it is something you already know: telling the time runs on exactly the numeral-bracket rule from
the numbers lesson, applied to the word час ("hour, o'clock").

## 1. The days of the week

<Compare
  columns={['Russian', 'English', 'Literally']}
  rows={[
    ['понедельник', 'Monday', 'after "не-деля" (the old word for Sunday / no-work day)'],
    ['вторник', 'Tuesday', 'from второй, "second"'],
    ['среда', 'Wednesday', 'from "middle" — the middle of the week'],
    ['четверг', 'Thursday', 'from четыре, "four"'],
    ['пятница', 'Friday', 'from пять, "five"'],
    ['суббота', 'Saturday', 'from "Sabbath"'],
    ['воскресенье', 'Sunday', 'from "resurrection"'],
  ]}
  caption="Four of the seven are built from numbers you learned last lesson, counting from Monday as day one."
/>

<Callout kind="note" title="Russian weeks start on Monday">
вторник is "the second day" and четверг "the fourth," which only works if Monday is day one — and
in Russian convention it is. Calendars, timetables and the week itself start on Monday, so
воскресенье (Sunday) is the *last* day, not the first.
</Callout>

**On** a given day uses **в** plus the accusative — and since most day names are masculine
inanimate or feminine, this is mostly painless:

<GrammarPoint pattern="в понеде́льник · в сре́ду · в суббо́ту" title="в + accusative = on (a day)">

в понедельник — "on Monday." Masculine day names don't change at all (inanimate accusative =
nominative), while feminine ones take the usual -а → -у ending: среда → в сре́ду, суббота →
в суббо́ту.

</GrammarPoint>

  ]}
  caption="All twelve are recognisably the same Latin-derived names English uses — the only work is the spelling and the stress. Note they are all masculine, and are not capitalised in Russian."
/>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Days and months are lowercase in Russian">
понедельник, январь — never Понедельник, Январь. Russian capitalises far less than English does:
days of the week, months, and nationality adjectives (ру́сский, from the introducing-yourself
lesson) all stay lowercase unless they begin a sentence.
</Callout>

## 3. Telling the time: the bracket rule again

<VocabCard word="час" stressed="час" meaning="hour; o'clock" />

To say what time it is, use Сейча́с ("now") plus the number plus час in whatever case that number
demands — the same three brackets as the numbers lesson:

<Compare
  columns={['Time', 'Russian', 'Why that form of час']}
  rows={[
    ["1 o'clock", 'Сейчас час.', 'The number 1 is simply dropped — час alone means "one o\'clock"'],
    ["2 o'clock", 'Сейчас два часа́.', '2–4 bracket → genitive singular часа́'],
    ["4 o'clock", 'Сейчас четыре часа́.', 'Still the 2–4 bracket'],
    ["5 o'clock", 'Сейчас пять часо́в.', '5–20 bracket → genitive plural часо́в'],
    ["11 o'clock", 'Сейчас одиннадцать часо́в.', 'Still the 5–20 bracket'],
  ]}
  caption="час / часа́ / часо́в isn't three vocabulary items to memorise — it's one noun obeying the bracket rule you already know."
/>

<Predict
  question="How would you say 'It's three o'clock'?"
  options={['Сейчас три час', 'Сейчас три часа́', 'Сейчас три часо́в', 'Сейчас три часы']}
  answer={1}
>
  Сейчас три часа́ — 3 falls in the 2–4 bracket, so час goes into the genitive singular, exactly
  as стол became стола́ after два.
</Predict>
