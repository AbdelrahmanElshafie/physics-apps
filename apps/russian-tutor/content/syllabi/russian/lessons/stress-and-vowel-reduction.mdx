---
title: Word stress and vowel reduction
summary: Stress isn't written down, lands on any syllable, and changes how unstressed vowels are even pronounced.
readingMinutes: 12
---

Russian spelling doesn't mark which syllable is stressed — you simply have to know, the same way
an English speaker has to already know that "record" is stressed differently as a noun
("RE-cord") than as a verb ("re-CORD"). The difference is that in Russian, *every* word has this
problem, and getting it wrong doesn't just sound foreign — it changes how the surrounding vowels
are pronounced too.

<Callout kind="note" title="How this course shows stress">
A lesson's reading line under each word (приве́т, спаси́бо) marks the stressed vowel with an accent
mark — a convention for learners, not something you'll see in ordinary Russian writing. Dictionaries
use the same convention for exactly this reason.
</Callout>

## 1. Stress can change the word entirely

Most of the time, stress just affects pronunciation. Occasionally, it's the only thing
distinguishing two completely different words that are spelled identically:

## 2. Unstressed о sounds like а

This is the single most common pronunciation shift in Russian, usually called **akanye**
("a-ing"). An unstressed **о** doesn't keep its "o" sound — it reduces toward an "a" sound, or an
even shorter neutral vowel the further it sits from the stress.

<VocabCard word="молоко" stressed="молоко́" meaning="milk" note={'Three written о\'s, but only the last one (stressed) is actually said as "o". The first two reduce toward "a".'} />

<Compare
  columns={['Written', 'Stress', 'Actually sounds closer to']}
  rows={[
    ['молоко́', 'last syllable', 'ma-la-KO'],
    ['хорошо́', 'last syllable', 'kha-ra-SHO'],
    ['до свида́ния', '3rd syllable of свидания', 'da svi-DA-nya'],
  ]}
  caption={'Every unstressed о in these words leans toward "a" in actual speech — spelling and pronunciation quietly part ways.'}
/>

<Callout kind="warning" title="The spelling never changes — only the sound does">
This is exactly the same kind of gap as Mandarin tone sandhi, if that's a useful comparison: a
predictable rule that changes *pronunciation*, while the *written word* stays exactly as it's
spelled. молоко is always spelled with three о's, no matter how it's actually said.
</Callout>

## 3. Unstressed е and я lean toward "i"

A similar, smaller shift happens to **е** and **я** when they're unstressed — they drift toward an
"i"-like sound rather than staying as crisp as their stressed version:
